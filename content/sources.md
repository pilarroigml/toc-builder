### Example 4: getting research into health policy decisions

The research topic (childhood vaccination) is invented for the example. The sources support how research uptake works, not any claim about vaccination methods.

**AI flag (6 October 2026, at Pilar's request):** the sources predate generative AI becoming part of everyday government work, which may change how evidence reaches decision makers (smaller teams, officials asking AI assistants instead of reading briefs, AI summaries that may misrepresent findings). The example adds an activity to publish data and plain summaries that AI tools can read, trains analysts to check AI summaries, and adds the assumption that AI tools represent the findings accurately. There is little evidence yet on how AI changes research uptake, so this part is a hypothesis and the example is marked "needs-review". Look for new evidence before v1 is shared widely.

## Approach to examples

All guidance examples follow one running story, keeping girls in secondary school, so readers can follow it from problem to impact. The examples are invented, but modelled on real programmes evaluated by impact evaluation teams (J-PAL, NBER researchers, IPA, World Bank and similar). The evaluation each one draws on is cited. No real organisation names or real figures are presented as the example's own.

## Open evidence gaps

- Household work as a reason girls leave school: **closed 6 October 2026** with UNICEF (global 2023, Latin America 2025, India 2024) and World Bank (Ghana water collection 2013). Most of this evidence shows an association rather than cause and effect, and the Zambia brief's data didn't list it among the leading reasons, so it remains context-specific. Example 1 moved from "needs-review" to "draft".
- Caregiver meetings on sharing household work: **replaced 6 October 2026** by family groups led by trained community facilitators with chiefs and religious leaders taking part, at Pilar's suggestion. Now supported by Population Council (Burkina Faso, Tanzania, 2020) and World Bank Economic Review (Zimbabwe, 2024). Their evidence is on girls' schooling and marriage. Neither measured household work directly, so the "sharing household work" part is still not evaluated.
- East Asia and the Pacific: no solid source found yet on household work and girls' schooling. The UNICEF Pacific gender page was checked and does not mention it.
