## World Bank Gender Innovation Labs, checked 6 October 2026

Tip for reading their briefs: each brief's page on documents.worldbank.org has a "TXT" link with a plain-text version, which avoids downloading the PDF.


- South Asia GIL impact evaluations, https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/world-bank-south-asia-region-gender-innovation-lab/impact-evaluations. Loads. Relevant: "Bus-tling to Success" (subsidised safe transport to middle school for rural girls, Pakistan, in progress, no results yet) and "SMS Girl" (texts to parents during COVID-19 school closures, Pakistan, results published). Transport study supports distance and safety as a recognised barrier. Revisit for results.
- East Asia and Pacific GIL, https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/east-asia-and-pacific-gender-innovation-lab. Loads. Its 2018 Indonesia research found that boys, not girls, had poorer schooling outcomes in Grade 8. A reminder that gaps don't always run the way people expect. Not cited yet.
- Africa GIL, https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/africa-gender-innovation-lab. Loads.
- Challenging conventional wisdom about girls' schooling (World Bank blog, Africa GIL, 2016), https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/voices/challenging-conventional-wisdom-about-girl-s-schooling. Briefly cited in the Problem tip, then removed at Pilar's request (6 October 2026). It's ten years old and later surveys exist, so its Liberia findings may be outdated. Replaced by a general note that evidence is context-specific.
- World Bank note "Determinants of the Basic Education Gender Gap in DRC: Supply and Demand Side Factors" (2021), https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/733861636744027227/pdf/Determinants-of-the-Basic-Education-Gender-Gap-in-DRC-Supply-and-Demand-Side-Factors.pdf. ## Approach to examples

All guidance examples follow one running story, keeping girls in secondary school, so readers can follow it from problem to impact. The examples are invented, but modelled on real programmes evaluated by impact evaluation teams (J-PAL, NBER researchers, IPA, World Bank and similar). The evaluation each one draws on is cited. No real organisation names or real figures are presented as the example's own.

## Open evidence gaps

- Household work as a reason girls leave school: **closed 6 October 2026** with UNICEF (global 2023, Latin America 2025, India 2024) and World Bank (Ghana water collection 2013). Most of this evidence shows an association rather than cause and effect, and the Zambia brief's data didn't list it among the leading reasons, so it remains context-specific. Example 1 moved from "needs-review" to "draft".
- Caregiver meetings on sharing household work (Activities strong example and Example 1). The problem is now evidenced, but this activity itself hasn't been evaluated in any source found. Worth flagging in expert review.
- East Asia and the Pacific: no solid source found yet on household work and girls' schooling. The UNICEF Pacific gender page was checked and does not mention it.
